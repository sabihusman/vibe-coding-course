## The build

- **What I built:** A rep's today view for a lightweight CRM companion. It puts today's follow-ups front and center, with 6 sample follow-ups, a big Log activity button, and a quick log for deal, outcome (Called, Emailed, Met, or No answer), next step, and date. A saved deal moves to Updated today, with Undo, and logging all 6 gives an All caught up state. Light mode is the default, with a dark option the user can choose. Sample data only, it resets on reload.
- **Tool used:** Muse
- **Shareable link:** https://muse.ai/s/crm-companion-today-view-xhxb64xvuxi42bb/Xz3LzvL8DBMydUOLV-UpqSR_Vz-VJKN_7a_qkCyfH1M

## Show & Swap read

_What a partner understood from your build with no verbal setup, their reaction is your first piece of product evidence._

- **What they understood immediately:** It is a quickly actionable dashboard, for everyday use, with prioritization of which tasks and customers to address first. It allows to check off tasks completed while also schedule the next one, great planner to make sure nothing falls through the cracks.
- **What confused them:** Not confusing but something to consider, prioritization of tasks can be set not only by due date but also by importance of account, for example considering ARR.
- **The assumption they thought you were testing:** Easy-to-action CRMs are more likely to be adopted, especially if they minimize the amount of work and jumps in between tools - for example it would be great if you could draft and send the email directly from the task to minimize the clicks.
- **The gap between what you intended and what they read:** I intended to test whether a one-tap today view makes logging fast enough that reps actually log between calls. My partner read it more as a daily planner that prioritizes work and keeps nothing from falling through the cracks. Their two suggestions point the same way: prioritization should weigh account importance, not just due date, and adoption may depend as much on cutting tool jumps, like drafting an email from the task, as on logging speed alone.
