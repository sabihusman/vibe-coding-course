# Validation Brief

> Module 2 · Validation. Frame the riskiest assumption, then build to test it. **This locks your scenario, no switching after M2.**

## Scenario

Scenario 02 · The Internal Tool Nobody Uses

## Riskiest assumption

_The one belief that, if false, sinks the idea._

The CRM doesn't fit how reps actually work, so they never adopt it. Specifically, reps will not do the call or email inside the CRM — they will keep using their usual email and phone, and log after, if at all.

## Hypothesis

> We believe **letting reps call or email right from the task** will cause **reps to stay in the CRM and log as they go, instead of doing the work outside it and logging after** for **sales reps, AEs, and SDRs**. We'll know we're right when **a partner does the call or email and the log from the same task, without leaving it, and says they would use it that way every day**.

## Risk type

- [ ] Value (do they want it?)
- [x] Usability (can they use it?)
- [ ] Feasibility (can we build it?)
- [ ] Viability (should we?)

## Kill switch

_The result that would make you stop or pivot._

If reps still leave the tool to call or email, or still prefer their spreadsheet after the redesign, the tool isn't the blocker, pivot. I will not keep polishing the log screen.

## The three ingredients

- **Real data (domain metrics):**
  - 18%, Weekly active adoption across licensed seats.
  - 8, Required fields to log a single deal update.
  - 11 min, Avg. task time for "update a deal" vs. 2-min target.
  - 63%, Reps keeping a shadow spreadsheet alongside the CRM.
- **User voice (verbatim quotes):**
  - "Logging activity feels like data entry for management, not something that helps me sell.", Senior AE
  - "If it could just tell me who to call next, I'd open it every morning.", SDR, occasional user
  - "It's faster to keep my deals in a spreadsheet than to fight the CRM's eight required fields.", Account exec, 2 logins / month
- **Hypothesis (above):** ✓
